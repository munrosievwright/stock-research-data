# Deep-Dive Research: ASML Holding NV (ASML) — 21 August 2026

*This is informational research, not personalized financial advice. It presents findings and reasoning for you to evaluate — it is not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold.*

## What the company does

ASML is the sole manufacturer of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, the highly specialized equipment required to produce the most advanced semiconductor chips. Every leading-edge chipmaker — including TSMC — depends on ASML equipment; it has no meaningful competitor at the cutting edge of the technology.

## Bull case

ASML's monopoly-like position in EUV lithography is arguably the single most durable competitive moat anywhere in the semiconductor supply chain — there is no viable alternative supplier for the equipment that makes leading-edge chips possible. The company's own fundamentals during this window were unambiguously strong: guidance was raised, not cut, and the High NA EUV milestone signals the next generation of the technology is progressing on schedule. Analyst sentiment remains a "Strong Buy" consensus with a meaningfully positive average target, and at least one major bank (BofA) has explicitly and publicly rejected the thesis that China poses a near-term threat to the business.

## Bear case / key risks

The China domestic-lithography narrative, even if currently more about long-term competitive positioning than an immediate earnings threat, is worth taking seriously precisely because it's the one scenario that could eventually erode ASML's structural moat — the market's sensitivity to this story (a 5.5%+ single-day move) suggests investors are already pricing in some probability of it mattering. The broader risk-off move in semiconductor equipment stocks after a strong run is a reminder that ASML, despite its unique competitive position, is not immune to sector-wide valuation resets. And the unresolved, undated dividend-increase detail recurring across multiple checks without confirmation is a small but real reminder to be skeptical of search-aggregated "facts" that can't be pinned to a specific date — a pattern worth remembering when evaluating any of this research.
