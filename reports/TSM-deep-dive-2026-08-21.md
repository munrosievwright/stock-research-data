## What the company does

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing chips designed by other companies (including NVIDIA, AMD, and Apple) rather than selling chips under its own brand. It is the critical manufacturing bottleneck for the entire advanced-chip industry — essentially every leading-edge AI accelerator is fabricated at a TSMC facility.

## Bull case

TSMC occupies a structurally advantaged position: it doesn't have to pick winners among AI chip designers because it manufactures for nearly all of them, capturing the industry's growth regardless of which specific company's chips ultimately win the most design wins. Its raised 2026 outlook (growth "slightly above 40%") and record Q2 results suggest AI-driven demand is translating directly into TSMC's own numbers, not just its customers'. The Arizona operation turning profitable is a meaningful de-risking of the "will U.S. expansion ever pay off" question that has weighed on the stock in past years. Analyst sentiment is essentially unanimous, and the average price target implies real additional upside from current levels.

## Bear case / key risks

The most-cited risk is structural, not company-specific: TSMC's most advanced manufacturing remains heavily concentrated in Taiwan, and cross-strait geopolitical tension is a recurring, non-diversifiable tail risk that no amount of strong quarterly execution fully resolves. Near-term, management itself flagged temporary gross-margin pressure from 2-nanometer ramp costs in the Q3 guide — a reminder that leading-edge node transitions carry real cost before they carry full revenue benefit. There are also generic overvaluation concerns noted alongside the otherwise optimistic analyst coverage, worth weighing against the stock's strong run and now-$2 trillion-plus valuation.
